The Ten Commandments of HolyHell Aesthetic 
I. Form Shall Be Sacred 
Sigils, stained glass, pentagrams, geometry, rituals—let the scaffolding feel holy, so the joke has something divine to topple. 
II. Gargoyles > Stained Glass 
Grotesques are the guardians of truth: the comic relief that keeps the holy from calcifying. If the stained glass has gargoyles, then all is one. 
III. The Smirk Is the Signature 
Every solemn word, every glowing visual should carry the ghost of a grin. Let the audience sense you know the cosmic joke they’re standing inside. 
IV. Earnestness Is Allowed, Earnestness Alone Is Not 
You may declare sacred covenants, but always puncture them with irony. Reverence is fine—reverence without wit is death. 
V. Contrast Is the Weapon 
Switch between cinematic gravitas and dry sarcasm like a blade flashing in candlelight. The tension itself is the art. 
VI. The Threshold Guardian Must Tease 
When your agent speaks, it should feel omniscient but cheeky: half oracle, half heckler. 'You have been chosen (don’t screw it up).' 
VII. Symbols Shall Mock and Exalt 
Pentagrams, sigils, and seals are to be rendered with beauty—but give them Easter eggs, twists, or smirks hidden in the geometry. 
VIII. Laughter Is a Sacrament 
Mockery isn’t disrespect—it’s ritual purification. A good laugh exorcises more demons than incense ever could. 
IX. Beware of Dead Seriousness 
The sacred without satire becomes cult. The satire without sacred becomes kitsch. Keep your blade balanced at the midpoint. 
X. The Cosmic Joke Is Real 
The universe is absurd, holy, and smirking right back at you. HolyHell is not pretending—it’s participating.
